My work aims to understand how planetary systems form and evolve, by finding and characterising **specific, well-chosen planets around bright, nearby stars**, which are the best targets for detailed follow-up studies. My main tool is **high-precision radial-velocity (RV) spectroscopy**, mostly with the SOPHIE spectrograph at the Observatoire de Haute-Provence and the SPIRou near-infrared spectropolarimeter at CFHT.

## Small planets around Sun-like and K stars

Large surveys have shown that super-Earths and sub-Neptunes are very common, often in compact multi-planet systems. Yet only a limited number of such low-mass planets are well characterised around nearby, bright stars. Radial velocities give precise masses and orbital eccentricities, which are key to constraining system dynamics, and they are the most efficient way to find **long-period planets** (beyond ~50 days), for which transits become rare.

I lead the follow-up of the **high-precision exoplanet programmes on SOPHIE**, and search for transits of our candidates with **TESS** and **CHEOPS**. Transiting planets around bright stars can then be studied in detail, for example by characterising their atmospheres with JWST.

A highlight is the **HD 88986** system ([Heidari, Boisse et al. 2024](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A%26A...681A..55H/abstract)): a cool sub-Neptune on a 146-day orbit, potentially transiting a naked-eye star, together with an outer giant companion.

## Complete architectures of planetary systems

Many planetary systems, including our own, host low-mass planets inside the orbit of giant planets. With G. Hébrard (IAP/OHP), I lead a SOPHIE programme searching for such small inner planets in systems where giant planets are already known. A second programme, with N. Hara (LAM) and A. Petit (Lagrange), aims to reveal the non-transiting companions of TESS planets: since transits require a specific geometry, RV follow-up is often the only way to uncover the **full architecture of a system** and constrain its formation history.

## Planets around M dwarfs

Super-Earths are extremely frequent around low-mass M dwarfs, often within the habitable zone. I take part in an intensive SOPHIE monitoring programme of M dwarfs (with IPAG), complementing the **SPIRou Legacy Survey** to improve phase coverage and to study the **chromatic behaviour of stellar activity** between the optical and the near-infrared. This work led, for example, to the detection of a potential super-Earth around **Gl 725 A**, an M dwarf in a binary system only 3.5 pc away ([Cortes-Zuleta, Boisse et al. 2025](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A%26A...693A.164C/abstract)).

## Circumbinary planets

Close binary stars shape the disc in which planets form, providing a different environment for planet formation. I collaborate on the **BEBOP** project (led by A. Triaud), whose northern sample is observed with SOPHIE. We made the first independent RV detection of the circumbinary planet **Kepler-16 (AB) b** and, more recently, the first circumbinary systems detected from the ground, such as **BEBOP-3 b**.

## Stellar activity and the limits of RV precision

Since my PhD, a core part of my expertise is identifying and correcting the instrumental and stellar noise that limits precise RVs. Stellar activity (spots, faculae, magnetic cycles) can mimic or hide planetary signals. I have extended this work to the **near-infrared**, where few activity indicators are known: searching for activity-sensitive spectral lines with SPIRou and NIRPS, and comparing optical and near-infrared behaviour ([Cortes-Zuleta, Boisse et al. 2023](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A%26A...673A..14C/abstract)). This work continues within the ANR project **ORVET** and the PLATO stellar-activity working group.

## Instrumentation

My work increasingly combines data analysis with instrument science:

- **SOPHIE-red** (PI and project lead): replacement of the SOPHIE camera, extending the wavelength range from 387–694 nm to about 379–900 nm with a gain in flux, to keep this workhorse instrument at the best level for the community beyond 2030.
- **ANDES @ ELT**: French representative on the Executive Board and coordinator of the French contributions to the high-resolution spectrograph for ESO's Extremely Large Telescope.
- **SPIRou**: former instrument scientist for the calibration unit, and contributions to the data-reduction tools.
- **NIRPS** (ESO 3.6 m, La Silla): science team member.

## Looking ahead

- Characterising the warm, long-period Neptune of the HD 88986 system with CHEOPS, HST and possibly JWST
- A new search for low-mass planets around **K dwarfs** with SOPHIE-red, attractive targets for PLATO
- Combining **Gaia DR4** astrometry with 20 years of SOPHIE data to better characterise giant planets and measure their occurrence
- Identifying nearby target stars for future **Earth-like planet characterisation** missions and instruments (ANDES, HWO, LIFE)
- Ground-based RV follow-up of **PLATO** transiting candidates with SOPHIE and SPIRou
